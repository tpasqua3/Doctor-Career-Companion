# Doctor Career Companion

A continuing-education and career app for one physician, built to be listened to. It writes lessons and board-style questions with AI, reads them aloud, tracks what has been mastered and what is slipping, and decides what to study next.

- **The app** (`app/companion.html`) is published as an artifact in Claude. Claude writes the lessons, questions, tutor answers and career plans.
- **The server** (`src/worker.js`, a Cloudflare Worker with a D1 database) holds accounts and the working copy of every study record, and copies each record into that account's own folder in Google Drive. The app inside Claude reaches it through a connector at `/mcp/<token>`.
- **The website** runs the same app at `/` (`public/index.html`, built from `app/companion.html` by `python3 tools/build.py`). Its AI is `/api/ai`, which can use four services: Gemini (`GEMINI_API_KEY`), Claude (`ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`), DeepSeek (`DEEPSEEK_API_KEY`) and Cloudflare Workers AI (the `AI` binding, shown as "Cloudflare AI Stack"). The services are one list in `src/worker.js` (`SERVICES`): each row names the key it needs, whether it may teach, and how to call it, and a service is offered only while its key is set. The app has two drop-downs built from that list: "Teaching by" at the top of every screen (services allowed to teach) and the chat choice in the Ask panel (all of them); both are also under Settings. when it is over its limit the next in `AI_ORDER` (default gemini, claude, llama) answers and the app says so. `AI_DAILY` caps requests per account per service per day (default 200; `AI_DAILY_GEMINI` and so on for one service). Models: `GEMINI_MODEL`, `GEMINI_MODEL_QUICK`, `AI_MODEL`, `AI_MODEL_QUICK`, `LLAMA_MODEL`. Inside Claude the app uses Claude and can send requests to the other services through the connector (`ask_ai`).
- **Who does what.** Every AI request names its job (lesson, lesson_lib, questions, review, plan, report, cards, notes, chat). `lesson_lib` is a lesson for which a library source was found: an assistant-tier service may write it when the user picks that service under "Teaching by", and the app then runs the lead review even if reviews are switched off. Settings shows, per writer, how its lessons, questions and cards held up at review. In `src/worker.js`, each service sits in a tier (lead, assistant, chat) and each job names the lowest tier allowed to do it (`TIERS`, `JOBS`, and `tier` on each `SERVICES` row). Assistant jobs go to assistants first, lead jobs follow the user's "Teaching by" choice, and a review goes to a service other than the writer whenever one exists. The owner moves services between tiers and changes a job's lowest tier on the account page; the changes are kept in D1 over the defaults. Settings in the app shows the resulting picture, and each lesson records which service wrote and reviewed it.
- **Privacy policy and terms of service** are served at `/privacy.html` and `/terms.html` from plain text. The starting wording is in `src/worker.js` (`PAGES`); the owner rewrites either page on the account page, and the edited version is kept in D1 and shown instead.
- **The account page** (`public/account.html`): sign up, reset or change a password, make the connector link, download a backup, see what is copied to Drive. The owner connects Google Drive and sees every account's status there.

After changing `app/companion.html`, run `python3 tools/build.py` before committing.

## What it does

- **Daily check-in.** Pick a commitment (8, 20, 40 or 60 minutes) and a practice setting (outpatient, mixed, inpatient). The planner picks topics from the record and lessons play back to back with the text following along on screen.
- **Audio first, text always.** Each lesson is written as a spoken script and shown as readable text. When the site has a `GOOGLE_TTS_KEY`, it is read by Google Cloud Text-to-Speech, staying inside the free monthly characters of each kind of voice in turn (Chirp 3 HD, then Neural2, then WaveNet; `googleSpeak` and `GTTS` in `src/worker.js`, 95% of each allowance unless `GOOGLE_TTS_SHARE` says otherwise), so the card on the Google account is not charged. After that, or without a key, it is read by a natural voice from Cloudflare's free voice model (`speak` in `src/worker.js`, reached as an `/api/ai` or `ask_ai` request whose job is `speak`; `TTS_DAILY_CHARS`, default 400,000 characters a day across all accounts, keeps it inside the free allowance). A passage that fails is asked for once more; two failures in a row rest the natural voice for two minutes and a real daily limit (a 429 from the server; a passing error is a 503) for thirty, and a note on screen says so. When that voice is used up, slow or unreachable, or the user picks it in Settings, the device's own voice reads instead, and Settings offers a choice among the device's voices. On the website a person can also download an offline voice (the open Kokoro model, Standard 92 MB or Full quality 326 MB) that then runs on their own device with no data use and no daily limit: `public/voice/worker.js` runs it, `public/voice/kokoro.web.js` is the kokoro-js library pointed at this site, and the model, voice and runtime files are kept in the R2 bucket `VOICE` and served from `/voice/hf/` and `/voice/ort/` (`voiceFile` in `src/worker.js`, which copies each file from its publisher the first time it is asked for). The reading order is offline voice if chosen and started, then the Cloudflare voice, then the device voice. Inside Claude the offline voice is not offered. While a lesson is being read, tapping its text moves the voice to the sentence that was tapped (`seekTap`). After a lesson the check questions can be read aloud with a pause before the answer, for hands-free use. **Pronunciations.** The Cloudflare voice takes plain text only and gets some words wrong, drug names above all. Settings has a Pronunciations card: a list of words, each with a spelling that makes that voice say it right, kept in the record as `says` (`{w, s, on}`). Just before a passage goes to the Cloudflare voice the server swaps each listed word for its respelling (`respell` in `src/worker.js`: whole words, any capitalisation, a plural s carried over); the Google voices and the text on screen are untouched. Each row has two play buttons that have the Cloudflare voice read the word as written and as respelled (a request whose job is `speak_cf`). While the Cloudflare voice is reading, the player shows a check-mark button. It pauses the lesson and opens the Pronunciation guide in the lower right, where the Ask panel opens (`drawFlag`): the list as a scrolling two-column table, Before and After, each side with a speaker button that has the Cloudflare voice read it, ending in an always-empty row to type a new word into; words of the passage being read are offered as chips; a slider makes the panel see-through, and the bar at its top is dragged to make it shorter or taller. Changes save as typed, and closing it reads the last passage again. The site owner's list (the account whose email is `OWNER_EMAIL`) is the central one and is used for every account; anyone else's list applies to their own account only, on top of the owner's, and wins where both give the same word (`sayFor`, `siteSays`). The app seeds the list with suggested respellings for common drug names (`SAYS0` in `app/companion.html`), each off until the listener has heard it. At the top of the card is the word tester: five likely misses shown as bubbles and a round arrow that shows five others. Tapping a bubble puts the word at the top of the list as a new entry and has the Cloudflare voice read it, so words can be tried without playing a lesson. The words are drug names and clinical terms found in the account's own lessons, cards and questions and in every library chapter or guideline the app reads (`sayHarvest`), terms an assistant picks for one curriculum topic at a time from that topic's library chapter (`sugTop`, job `notes`), and a starter list (`SAYP0`); a word already listed or tried before (`saytry` in the record) is not offered again. A list may hold up to 2,000 words in use. The list itself now lives in the **Pronunciation test** pop-up that Settings opens (`spOpen`): the word tester on top, then the whole list as a Before/After table, newest first, with a find box. Each row has two checks: on the left, "sounds right as written" (the row is removed and noted as `asis`), and on the right, "the respelling sounds right". When a word is added the AI suggests a respelling (`guessAsk`, job `notes`), written after studying the listener's own confirmed respellings, the AI suggestions they kept or changed, and the words the voice already says right; those notes are kept in the record as `saylearn` (`{w, g: the suggestion, s: what was kept, r: ok | edit | asis}`). A suggestion is off (shown dashed) until it is confirmed with the right-hand check, so an unheard guess is never used in a lesson.
- **Boards.** A track built from the ABIM internal medicine blueprint: every content category with its share of the exam and every topic with its relevance rating by task. The planner weights topics by relevance and exam share, drops topics rated low, and reports a readiness score. Two outlines come with the app, so Boards works on the website and inside Claude with no setup: cert (the first-time Certification exam: every examinable topic with the exam share of its category and subsection, no ratings) and recert (the MOC exam and LKA: every topic rated by task). The physician picks one on the Boards tab; sessions, questions, cards and the plan follow it, and progress carries across by topic name. On the Boards tab a different edition can be uploaded as the ABIM's PDF (read in the browser by where the text sits on the page), or loaded from Google Drive inside Claude; it is kept in the physician's record and is offered as a third choice.
- **Companion and learning plan.** An Ask button in the corner of every screen opens one conversation that knows what is on screen, the study record, the blueprint relevance and the matching reference chapter. It builds a self-paced plan for the whole curriculum, laid out by the app in weekly pages so its length is not limited by one AI reply (the AI adds the roadmap and chooses what comes first): the blueprint sets the frame, and within it priority goes to demonstrated uncertainty (wrong answers, low self-ratings, cards rated low, topics asked about, named gaps).
- **Requests and academic half day.** Any topic can be requested by name and is taught, tested or carded on the spot, filed under the matching curriculum topic. A schedule of the residency program's teaching sessions moves each topic to the front of the plan in the week before.
- **Study log and feedback loop.** Lessons can be marked complete; every lesson, question set and deck is logged with the time spent. Questions put to the companion are kept as a short history and a running AI note on what the physician is trying to understand. All of it feeds one need figure per topic that orders sessions, shapes the plan and shortens flashcard intervals on weak topics; card ratings feed back into mastery.
- **Landscape and in-training exam.** Disciplines are scored in points and placed against the physician's own average. An in-training exam report (PDF, pictures or pasted text) is read into content-area scores and missed objectives, which are matched to topics and raised in the plan.
- **Curriculum.** Four tracks: internal medicine by body system, osteopathic manipulative medicine, procedures and regenerative medicine (office procedures, point-of-care ultrasound, shockwave, functional medicine), and practice (quality measures, coding, EHR optimization for Epic, Oracle Health/Cerner, MEDITECH and others).
- **Questions.** ABIM-style single best answer, written per topic or for a knowledge gap stated in the physician's own words. Each set passes a second AI review that drops any item with an arguable key before it is shown. Misses return in 1, 3, 7 and 21 days.
- **Cards.** Short high-yield flashcards per topic, written on request and added by every lesson. Flip, rate confidence on a five-point scale, and each card returns on a spaced schedule. Decks can be flipped, read as a list or listened to; views are counted per day.
- **Backup.** The History tab (and Settings) downloads a backup file of the whole record and restores one from an uploaded file.
- **Mastery.** Per topic: questions answered, percent correct, lessons, self-rating, last studied. A 0–100 score blends smoothed accuracy, the amount of evidence, the self-rating and time since review. Confidence that outruns accuracy is flagged.
- **Consistency.** Streaks, days active per week and month, minutes per day, week and month, listening share.
- **Reference library.** Inside Claude, clinical lessons, questions and cards are grounded in the user's own texts in Google Drive: a board review syllabus as the first source and optional textbook folders as a second, each with an edition year so time-sensitive content follows the most recent source. Figures in a chapter (a title, the image, its legend) can be shown beside the lesson section that covers exactly that finding. Text and images are read when needed and are never stored by the app or this repository; each user connects their own library.
- **Guideline PDFs.** A folder named Guidelines inside the library is read as published guidelines. The app's own PDF reader (pdf.js in the browser) rebuilds each page from where its text and drawn shapes sit: columns are read in order, tables are kept out of the prose and transcribed row by row, a `>` with a stroke drawn under it is read as `≥`, and symbol-font glyphs are read as the signs they print. A captioned table or figure can be shown beside the lesson segment or flashcard that teaches it, drawn from its own region of the source page, with the guideline's citation. Before one is shown it must be chosen for that segment, share most of its title's words with it, pass the review pass, and, inside Claude, pass a look at the cropped picture. A registry file in the library (JSON with `sources[].drive_file_id`, label, citation, status) supplies citations and keeps superseded guidelines out. The website gets the PDF from `/api/drive/file`; inside Claude a small PDF comes through the Google Drive connector and a large one in slices through this app's connector (`library_file`), else the file is read as plain text.
- **Career.** Goals by area, each with an AI-reasoned plan: steps, measures, risks and the facts to look up.

## Accuracy

Content is generated, not hand-written. The prompts require named guidelines, forbid invented citations and numbers, and ask for an explicit list of anything to verify. The in-app AI has no live web access, so recent guideline changes can be missing. Flagging a question removes it from the statistics.

## Deploy

Cloudflare dashboard → Workers & Pages → Create → Import a repository → this repo. Every push to `main` deploys. The D1 database is created on the first deploy. Optional: a `SIGNUP_CODE` secret limits sign-up to people who are given that code (without it, anyone with the address can create an account and use the site's AI allowance). Optional: a `RESEND_API_KEY` secret (and `MAIL_FROM`) turns on password reset by email.

### Passwords

- **Change password** (logged in, Account card on the account page): `POST /api/password` with the current and the new password. That device stays logged in; every other session of the account ends.
- **Forgot password** on the login screen works one of two ways, reported by `GET /api/reset/mode`:
  - `direct` (the default while no `RESEND_API_KEY` is set): `POST /api/forgot/questions` takes the account's email and returns the recovery questions that account set; `POST /api/forgot/direct` takes the email, one of those questions, its answer and the new password. Each account sets one to three questions from the list (`QUESTIONS` in `src/worker.js`) in the Recovery questions card on the account page (`PUT /api/recovery`, current password required); answers are kept only as salted hashes in the `recovery` table. An account with no questions set is checked against the first and last name it was created with instead, so until it sets one, anyone who knows its email and name can replace its password. Five wrong tries lock the account for 15 minutes.
  - `email` (as soon as `RESEND_API_KEY` is set): `POST /api/forgot` sends a one-hour single-use link (`resets` table), and `POST /api/reset` takes the token and the new password. The direct form then switches itself off.
- `PASSWORD_RESET` (a variable) overrides this: `direct` keeps the direct form on alongside email, `off` switches the direct form off.
- All three paths end in `setPassword` in `src/worker.js`.

### Google Drive: the knowledge folder and the account folders

The server touches two Drive folders, set by id in `wrangler.jsonc`:

- `DRIVE_KNOWLEDGE_FOLDER`: the reference library. Read only. Every file asked for is checked to sit inside this folder before it is read. `LIBRARY_EMAILS` (a secret) lists the account emails that may read it, or `*` for every account.
- `DRIVE_ACCOUNTS_FOLDER`: one subfolder per account, made when the account is created: named by a random code only (`acct-…`, no name or email in the folder or its files): `account.json`, `records/` (one file per record) and `backups/` (one dated file per day of activity, newest 14 kept). D1 stays the copy the app reads; each save is copied to Drive after the reply, and a five-minute timer copies whatever is waiting.

Accounts are kept apart by the server, not by the AI: the account comes from the login or connector link, its folder comes from its own row in D1, and no request names a folder. AI services hold no Google credential. When Gemini is allowed to look things up (the Ask companion), the server runs each lookup against the caller's rows only.

Setup: in Google Cloud create an OAuth client (type Web application) with the redirect address `https://<your site>/api/google/callback`, enable the Drive API, and add the secrets `GOOGLE_OAUTH_CLIENT_ID`, `GOOGLE_OAUTH_CLIENT_SECRET` and `OWNER_EMAIL`. Then log in as the owner and press Connect Google Drive on the account page. The token is kept encrypted in D1. A `GOOGLE_SERVICE_ACCOUNT` key still works for reading the library, but Google does not let a service account keep files in a personal Drive, so it cannot write account folders.

Google Docs are read as text and their figures shown; PDFs and other files are converted with Workers AI. Library text is read when needed and never written to the database.

Then, on the site's account page, make a connector link and add it in Claude as a custom connector named **Doctor Companion App**.
